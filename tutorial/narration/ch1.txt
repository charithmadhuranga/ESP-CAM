The target is the AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM. It pairs the original ESP32 chip with an OV2640 camera sensor, four megabytes of external PSRAM, and a micro SD card slot.

Programming happens over the ESP32-CAM-MB adapter, which gives the board a USB serial port.

The firmware is built with PlatformIO on native ESP-IDF version six point one. There is no Arduino layer, and every version is pinned, so builds are reproducible.

The four megabyte flash chip is divided by a partition table. Two app slots of one and a half megabytes each leave room for safe over-the-air updates later.

A small NVS area stores settings and credentials, and a raw storage partition is reserved for future models.

With every feature included, the firmware fills about eighty three percent of one app slot, which still leaves room to grow.

The SD card runs in one-bit mode. That keeps the flash LED pin, which this board shares with the card, out of the data path.

On every boot, the firmware checks PSRAM, starts the camera, mounts the card, runs a quick read and write self-test, and then brings up Wi-Fi and the web server.
